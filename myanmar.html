Central Myanmar

Bagan

2–3 days recommended

Over 2,000 Buddhist temples scattered across a single plain, best seen from a hot air balloon at first light or a bicycle at dusk.

Why it's special: Nowhere else in Southeast Asia packs this density of centuries-old temples into a walkable, cyclable plain — it rewards slow, unplanned wandering as much as any guided stop.

Top Attractions

  • Ananda Temple's gilded stupa
  • Shwesandaw Pagoda sunset terrace
  • Dhammayangyi, Bagan's largest temple
  • Old Bagan archaeological zone

Recommended Activities

Sunrise balloon ride E-bike temple hopping River sunset cruise Lacquerware workshop

Famous Local Foods

Mohinga (fish noodle soup) Tea leaf salad Shan noodles Palm jaggery sweets

Culture & Traditions

Bagan remains an active pilgrimage site — locals still leave offerings at working monasteries between the ruins. Modest dress and bare feet are expected at every temple, and quiet respect goes further than any camera angle.

Best Time to VisitNovember – February

Lower Myanmar

Yangon

2 days recommended

Myanmar's largest city — colonial-era streets, colorful street-food alleys, and the gold spire of Shwedagon Pagoda visible from almost anywhere.

Why it's special: Yangon is most travelers' first and last stop, and it earns both roles — a compact, walkable downtown with enough texture for a full two days on its own.

Top Attractions

  • Shwedagon Pagoda at sunset
  • Colonial downtown architecture walk
  • Bogyoke Aung San Market
  • Kandawgyi Lake boardwalk

Recommended Activities

Circular train ride Street food crawl Gem & jade market visit Heritage walking tour

Famous Local Foods

Mohinga Ohn no khao swè Grilled skewers, 19th Street Burmese milk tea

Culture & Traditions

Evening prayer at Shwedagon draws locals daily, not just tourists — arrive an hour before sunset to watch the pagoda fill with candlelight. Shoes and socks come off at the gate, no exceptions.

Best Time to VisitNovember – February

Shan State

Inlay Lake

2 days recommended

A highland lake ringed by mountains, home to stilt-house villages, floating gardens, and fishermen who row standing on one leg.

Why it's special: Life here genuinely happens on the water — markets, farms and workshops all float or stand on stilts, making a boat ride the whole day's itinerary rather than one stop on it.

Top Attractions

  • Floating gardens & villages
  • Indein's leaning stupa fields
  • Phaung Daw Oo Pagoda
  • Five-day rotating market

Recommended Activities

Sunrise boat tour Weaving & silversmith visits Cycling the lake shore Hot air balloon (Oct–Mar)

Famous Local Foods

Shan-style tofu Lake fish curry Shan noodle salad Highland avocado

Culture & Traditions

The Intha people's leg-rowing style developed to keep hands free for fishing nets — it's a livelihood, not a performance, so guides ask visitors not to request poses for photos.

Best Time to VisitOctober – March

Beach Luxury

Rakhine Coast

Ngapali Beach

2–4 days recommended

A quiet, palm-lined stretch of the Bay of Bengal — Myanmar's answer to a slow beach week, with far fewer crowds than its Thai or Balinese counterparts.

Why it's special: Development here stayed deliberately low-rise, so the beach itself — not a strip of bars — is still the main event, best paired at the end of a Bagan-Inle-Yangon loop.

Top Attractions

  • Ngapali's main beach stretch
  • Lin Thar fishing village
  • Thandwe town & market
  • Pearl Island snorkeling spot

Recommended Activities

Snorkeling boat trip Beachside massage Sunset horseback ride Seafood dinner on the sand

Famous Local Foods

Grilled tiger prawns Rakhine fish curry Mont di (fish noodle) Fresh coconut water

Culture & Traditions

Fishing crews still launch at dawn from the same beach guests relax on by afternoon — a reminder this is a working coastline first, resort second.
